<section class="agp-section">
<h2 class="agp-h2">The idea</h2>
<div class="agp-manifesto">
<p>Most agent frameworks today are really platforms. Your runs, traces and data live on their side, and the bill grows with every one of them. Developers recognise that shape: it's a trap.</p>
<p>And it's not only the bill. When prompts and customer data sit on someone else's servers, security teams, compliance officers and regulators start asking hard questions: where is the data, who can see it, can it leave the country?</p>
<p>AGBoss takes the path Spring took for Java: a <strong>DSL, not a platform</strong>, made just for agents. It runs inside your backend, with the model provider you choose, so your data stays inside your perimeter. You declare components; the framework wires, tests and traces them, and tells you what they cost.</p>
<p>And it's <strong>open source, all of it</strong>. Developers should use AGBoss because it's convenient and it works — not because leaving is expensive.</p>
</div>
</section>
